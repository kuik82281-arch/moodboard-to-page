# The twelve dimensions of a style

Read every reference along these. Write what you **see** (concrete, visible) and what it **means for UI**.

| # | Dimension | Questions to answer |
|---|-----------|---------------------|
| 1 | **Material** | What are things made of? Paper (which: newsprint, kraft, rice paper, photocopy), fabric, metal, glass, acrylic, plastic, lacquer, porcelain, wood, screen. Is it matte, glossy, translucent? |
| 2 | **Edges** | How do shapes end? Torn, burnt, scissor-cut, deckled, die-cut, perfectly round, sharp, scalloped, framed, scroll ends, seal borders. |
| 3 | **Layering** | Are things stacked, overlapping, taped, pinned, floating, or laid out apart? Tilted or straight? How many layers deep? |
| 4 | **Density** | Full to the edges, or lots of empty space? Where is it busy, where calm? |
| 5 | **Text as texture** | Is there text used as decoration (newsprint columns, handwriting, graffiti, calligraphy, slogans, tiny labels)? In which languages, how faint, which direction? |
| 6 | **Typography** | The personality of display and body type: newspaper serif, didone, script, blackletter, condensed grotesque, ransom-note cut-outs, round sans, thin sans, brush calligraphy, Song / Kai, vertical setting. Sizes and contrast between them. |
| 7 | **Colour** | Palette with roles (ground, ink, accent), saturation, temperature, contrast; how colour ages or glows. |
| 8 | **Motifs** | Recurring objects and ornaments (clocks, instruments, flourishes / safety pins, stars, skulls / bears, hearts, ribbons / clouds, lanterns, plum branches, fret borders). |
| 9 | **Light & depth** | Flat, printed, embossed, cast shadows, glow, sheen, grain, halftone. |
| 10 | **Motion** | If it moved, how: still, fluttering paper, jittery xerox, bouncy soft, slow ink-wash. |
| 11 | **Breakage** | Where the style breaks things (tears, burns, cuts, scribbles, misregistration) and where it insists on wholeness, order, symmetry. How much of the page may be broken. |
| 12 | **Integration** | How content belongs to its medium in the references: printed / written / stitched / carved *into* the ground, or pasted / pinned *onto* it, or floating above it. Which parts of the UI are "made in the medium" (a newspaper calendar is a boxed feature printed on the page, in ink, on the same paper) and which are added objects (scraps, stickers)? Where is the rhythm of filled and empty space, and how random is the placement of added things? |
